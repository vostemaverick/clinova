Anemia - Complete Notes (With Labs & Doses)

1. Definition

Hb below normal for age/sex: Men <13 g/dl, Women <12 g/dl, Pregnant <11 g/dl, Child <11 g/dl. Results in reduced O2 delivery to tissues.

2. Classification - This is what lecturers test

MCVTypeCauses (Kenya Common)Key Lab
<60-80MicrocyticIron deficiency (most common), Thalassemia, Sideroblastic, Chronic diseaseLow Ferritin, Low Serum Iron
80-100NormocyticAcute bleeding, CKD, Aplastic anemia, HemolysisRetic high if bleeding/hemolysis
>100MacrocyticB12 deficiency, Folate deficiency, Alcohol, Hypothyroid, Drugs (MTX)High MCV, Hypersegmented neutrophils

3. Pathophysiology

  • Iron Def: No iron → no heme → small pale RBCs. Cause in Kenya: Hookworms, poor diet, menorrhagia, pregnancy
  • B12/Folate: Needed for DNA synthesis → RBC nucleus doesn't mature → big RBCs (megaloblastic)
  • Hemolysis: RBCs destroyed early (sickle cell, malaria, autoimmune) → marrow tries to compensate → retics ↑

4. Clinical Features

General: Fatigue, pallor (conjunctiva, palms), dizziness, palpitations, SOB on exertion, headache, tinnitus

Specific signs:

  • Iron: Spoon nails (koilonychia), angular cheilitis, pica (eating soil)
  • B12: Glossitis (beefy tongue), peripheral neuropathy, subacute combined degeneration of cord
  • Hemolysis: Jaundice, splenomegaly, dark urine
  • Severe: Heart failure, tachycardia, systolic murmur

5. Investigations - Stepwise

Step 1: CBC + MCV + Retic count → Classify by MCV
Step 2: If microcytic → Serum Ferritin, Iron studies, Stool for occult blood & ova (hookworm)
Step 3: If macrocytic → B12, Folate, TSH, LFTs
Step 4: If hemolysis suspected → LDH ↑, Bilirubin ↑ (indirect), Haptoglobin ↓, Blood film (sickle cells, spherocytes)
Always in Kenya: Malaria test, HIV test, Stool microscopy

6. Management (Kenya MOH Guidelines)

A) Iron Deficiency:
Ferrous sulphate 200mg TDS (60mg elemental iron) x 3 months AFTER Hb normal. Give with Vitamin C, empty stomach. Counsel about black stool & constipation. Treat cause - deworm with Albendazole 400mg stat, treat menorrhagia.
Diet: Liver, beans, green veggies, fortified flour
If severe/intolerant: Iron sucrose IV

B) Megaloblastic:
B12 deficiency: Hydroxocobalamin 1mg IM daily x 5 days then monthly. If diet related - oral B12 50mcg daily.
Folate: Folic acid 5mg daily x 4 months. NEVER give folate alone if B12 low - will worsen neuro damage

C) Sickle Cell / Hemolysis:
Folic acid 5mg daily lifelong, Hydroxyurea, Avoid triggers, Transfuse if crisis
When to Transfuse? (Not every low Hb!)
- Hb <4 g/dl regardless of symptoms
- Hb <5 g/dl with signs of heart failure
- Hb <6 g/dl in pregnancy
- Symptomatic + ongoing bleeding
Use packed cells, not whole blood. Transfuse slowly + give Furosemide to avoid overload.

🔥 OSCE / Viva Must-Know

Q: Woman Hb 8, MCV 68, Ferritin low. Diagnosis & why pica?
A: Iron deficiency anemia. Pica due to iron deficiency - craving for non-food.

Q: How to differentiate Iron def vs Thalassemia both microcytic?
A: Iron def: Low ferritin, high RDW. Thalassemia: Normal ferritin, normal RDW, family history, Mentzer index <13

Past Paper Chuka 2024: "30yr old vegan with fatigue, tingling feet, MCV 110" - Answer = B12 deficiency from vegan diet → do B12 level, give IM B12.
