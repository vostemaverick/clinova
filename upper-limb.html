Upper Limb Anatomy - Complete & Clinical

1. Bones & Joints (Quick Recall)

Shoulder: Clavicle, Scapula (glenoid), Humerus (head). Joint = Ball & socket - most mobile, most dislocated.
Elbow: Humerus + Ulna + Radius - Hinge joint.
Wrist: Radius + Carpals - 8 carpal bones.

Carpals: She Looks Too Pretty Try To Catch Her - Scaphoid, Lunate, Triquetral, Pisiform, Trapezium, Trapezoid, Capitate, Hamate. Scaphoid = most fractured carpal → Tenderness in anatomical snuffbox.

2. Brachial Plexus - The Big Thing

3. Arterial Supply & Clinical

Subclavian → Axillary → Brachial → Radial & Ulnar → Palmar arches. Brachial artery is where you check BP and feel for pulse in cubital fossa. Radial artery for pulse at wrist. Allen's test before radial puncture.

4. Important Spaces & Clinical Correlates

  • Anatomical snuffbox: Scaphoid + Radial artery - Tenderness = scaphoid fracture → Risk AVN
  • Cubital fossa: Contains (lateral to medial) TNBT - Tendon biceps, Nerve median, Brachial artery, Tendon? Actually Brachialis. Good for blood draw.
  • Carpal tunnel: Median nerve compression → Night pain, tingling thumb-index-middle. Test: Tinel, Phalen.

Erb's Palsy vs Klumpke's - Favorite Exam:
Erb's (C5,6): Waiter's tip - arm adducted, internally rotated, elbow extended, forearm pronated. After shoulder dystocia.
Klumpke's (C8,T1): Claw hand + Horner syndrome. After breech or arm pulled.

🔥 OSCE Station: Upper Limb Exam

1. Look: Wasting (thenar/hypothenar), scars, clawing
2. Feel: Pulse, temperature
3. Move: Active then passive, test abduction, flexion/extension
4. Nerves: Test sensation - Axillary regimental badge, Median palmar thumb, Ulnar little finger, Radial dorsal 1st web
5. Special tests: Tinel, Phalen, Froment
Past Paper: "Patient can't extend wrist after humeral fracture" - Answer: Radial nerve injury in spiral groove → Wrist drop → Give cock-up splint + physio

Memory trick: Radial = Wrist DROP, Median = Ape hand + Benediction, Ulnar = Claw, Axillary = Can't abduct
